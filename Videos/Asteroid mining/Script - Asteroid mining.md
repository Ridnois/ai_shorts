# Script - Asteroid mining

← [[YouTube Channel]] · [[Asteroid mining - Status]] · [[Guidelines for new scripts]] · [[Narration guide]] · [[Shorts quality bar]]

**Request (user, 5 Oct 2026):** *"how complex asteroid mining would really be, from start to end"*; the agent decides and executes.
**Question (one sentence):** what are the steps of a real asteroid mine, and which of them is nobody close to solving?
**Theme line:** *flying to an asteroid is the easy part; landing, digging, refining and selling are the problems.*

Scores are the agent's guess. **Coffee test:** "We have brought home 127 grams of asteroid in total, and a real mine has four steps nobody has solved." A friend would ask "which four?", so it stays ≥ 4. **Street-lamp test:** a headline ("asteroids are worth trillions") would not answer it. **Expert test:** each scene carries a detail a generic explainer skips (a sampler that sank, no refining in zero gravity, the price crash). The ball pit became the turn (scene 4), the myth and the startups were cut.

## Hooks (five; chosen: 1)

1. **"We've brought home just 127 grams of asteroid."** (chosen: a checkable claim in plain words; the reason, a billion dollars, follows at once)
2. "A real asteroid mine has four steps, and three are unsolved."
3. "You could jump off an asteroid, and that is why you can't mine one."
4. "An asteroid probe once sank into its target like a ball pit."
5. "Mining an asteroid is harder than reaching it."

Spoken figures: 127 g, one billion dollars, 170 tonnes (3). Facts: 127 g and cost, fuel, gravity and the ball pit, no zero-g refining, the platinum market (5). Cut test: removed the Psyche myth, Planetary Resources, Odin, Hayabusa2's 5.4 g as a separate fact, the amino acids, the legal framework. Captions show the figures as figures ("127", "170") through `captions` overrides.

**Claims to attribute carefully:** "less fuel than the Moon's surface" = *some* near-Earth asteroids (5.5 against 6.3 km/s from low Earth orbit); "could crash the price" is an economist's warning, not a measurement; "most plans start with water" is the agent's reading of the sources (AstroForge is the counter-example) and is worded "probably" and "most plans".
