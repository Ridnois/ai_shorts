# Script - Moon

← [[YouTube Channel]] · [[Moon - Status]] · [[The Moon is leaving us]] · [[Guidelines for new scripts]] · [[Narration guide]] · [[Shorts quality bar]]

**Question (plain):** the Moon is slowly leaving us, so how close was it at the start, and what has that done to our days?
**Theme line (rule 0.9):** *the Moon was born about twenty times closer, and its leaving is what keeps stretching Earth's day.*
**Choice:** the user picked option C of [[The Moon is leaving us]] ("The Moon once loomed in the sky"): *"first theme is attractive, a mirror left on the moon is boring"*.

## Cut test and checks

- Removed from the first draft: the fossil-day count (400 days a year), the 70 % distance step, the eclipse remark, tidal locking.
- Bridges: every scene opens by tying back ("so close", "those tides", "the tug", "it is still going", "so the day keeps stretching").
- Ending: a concrete payoff (*the day keeps stretching because the Moon keeps leaving; 24 hours is only today's length*), not an aphorism.
- Plain-words test on scene 1: "newborn Moon" and "twenty times closer" need no definition.
- Rigor: "twenty times" is the model start (≈ 16,200 km against 385,000 km, ratio ≈ 24, rounded down); the card says "model". "Six hundred million years ago, about twenty-two hours" is the cited estimate (21.9 ± 0.4 h). "Almost two milliseconds" is 1.72.
