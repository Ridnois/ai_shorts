## 1. Idea gate

**Street-lamp test: the original angle fails.** "Desert sand is too round" is the headline that the 2.9M-view competitor video already gives (MLTW Facts, [[Niche map]]), and fact 10 shows the strong version is wrong: dune sand **can** be used, with strength loss under 25 % at full replacement. **Revised angle (needs the user's yes before P2):** *the real limit is the right sand in the right place, not sand itself.* The mechanism beyond the headline: rounded and very fine grains do not just "not stick", they change cost and durability (more paste and water, more absorption, facts 10, 14, 15), so builders pay to import the better grain, while the world's use is 50 billion tonnes and US prices rose about 38 % (facts 1, 6, 8). That also fixes honesty: the title cannot say "cannot". **Fact 14 and 15 must be read before scripting.**

**Scores (revised thread):** surprise 4 · scale 4 · depth 4 · evidence 3 (three key facts unread; secondary UAE figure) · showable 4 · plain 5. Surprise, scale, plain all ≥ 4. Rejected, one line each: **"Dubai's sand from Australia"** (no primary source); **sand mining crime and ecology** (other video, not read); **"cannot build"** (false by fact 10).
**Against the idea (three reasons to scroll past):** (1) "I know this, it is round grains": answered only by the cost-not-impossible turn; (2) "sand is boring": answered by 50 billion tonnes and the price rise; (3) "not my problem": weak unless the close ties it to the concrete in a viewer's city. If the user rejects the turn, the idea scores surprise 3 and drops.
**Coffee test:** *"Desert sand actually can make concrete, it just does it worse and pricier, so the world is short of the right sand."* "Huh, so there is no shortage of sand?" Surprise 4.
**Expert test:** a materials engineer would say the interesting part is gradation and paste demand, not roundness alone. Pass if fact 14 and 15 are read.
