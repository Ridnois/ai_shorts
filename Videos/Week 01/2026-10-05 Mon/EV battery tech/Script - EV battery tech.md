## 1. Scoring (1 to 5; the agent's guess)

| Candidate thread | Surprise | Scale | Depth | Evidence | Showable | Plain |

| **A · Wear is slow (2%) and charging power beats heat; cold costs range, not life; LFP made packs cheap** (chosen) | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 5 |

**Chosen: A**, with the plating mechanism (C) as the explainer for "why charging hard wears it", D as the cost close. Rejected, one line each: **B** is mostly company claims (CATL, BYD) and dates fast (the user can swap it in as a separate video); **C** alone is too technical for a plain opener; **E** is warranty fine print. **Coffee test:** *"Electric car batteries only lose about 2% a year, and how fast you charge matters more than heat."* A friend would say "huh, I thought hot weather killed them": surprise 4. **Street-lamp test: borderline** ("EV batteries last long and got cheap" is a headline); rescued by the mechanism and the counter-intuitive ranking (charging power over climate, cold costs range not life). **Expert test:** plating on graphite, the 80% state-of-charge condition and the heater share of winter loss are what a generic explainer skips.

## 2. Five hooks

1. **"Electric car batteries lose about two percent a year."** (plain, checkable, opens "so how long?" and "what makes it worse?") **chosen**
2. "Fast charging wears a battery twice as fast." (strong but narrower)
3. "Cold weather cuts an electric car's range by a third." (true only with the heater on: needs the caveat)
4. "A car battery now costs about a third of what it did." (unsourced, rejected)
5. "Your electric car's worst enemy is not the weather." (a tease, no claim)

Test: *would I stop?* (1: "only two percent? I was told they die in five years") *does it open a question the next scene answers?* (yes).

## 5. Sources and what was not verified

**Read in full:** Geotab's press release (13 Jan 2026) and BloombergNEF's pack-price release (Dec 2025). **Search summaries only:** the AAA cold-weather tests (a May 2026 report relayed by Autoblog and others; the 39% includes the cabin heater and a 20 °F test), the heat-pump split, the plating and SEI papers (the PNAS PDF could not be parsed), Tesla's 12%, the warranty rule, and every CATL and BYD claim (company figures, not used in the voice). **Not verified:** the exact AAA protocol (the "most of that is the cabin heater" is the summaries' reading: 10 to 12% from temperature alone, the rest from climate control); that Geotab's 2.3% is a mean (it does not give a floor); that BNEF's $108 is volume-weighted global (its own words).
