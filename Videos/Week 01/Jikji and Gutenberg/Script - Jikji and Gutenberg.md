## 1. Idea gate

| Thread                                      | Surprise | Scale | Depth | Evidence | Showable | Plain |
| ------------------------------------------- | -------- | ----- | ----- | -------- | -------- | ----- |
| **A · The mistakes are the proof** (chosen) | 4        | 4     | 4     | 4        | 4        | 4     |

Rejected, one line each: **B** "Korea beat Gutenberg" is the headline (street-lamp); **C** the book's trip to Paris is a story, not a mechanism; **D** "why Gutenberg won" rests on one sentence of one article, kept only as the turn; **E** how the type was cast has a single weak source (fact 16).

**Street-lamp test: pass.** The headline answers "who was first"; it does not answer "how do we know". The mechanism (a carved block cannot turn a letter over, loose pieces can) is what the video adds. **Coffee test:** *"A Korean book from 1377 has an upside-down letter, which proves it was printed with loose metal type, seventy years before Gutenberg."* A friend says "oh, that's how you can tell": surprise 4. **Expert test:** each scene has a detail a generic explainer skips (the mistake itself, block versus pieces, the postscript, 1972, free online). **Against the idea (three reasons to scroll):** (1) "I already know Korea was first" → the video does not argue priority, it shows the evidence; (2) "Gutenberg was not first" sounds like clickbait → scenes 2 to 5 deliver exactly that claim, scoped to metal-type books; (3) few photos show the real pages → the pages are public-domain on Commons and the gaps are covered by three drawn explainers.

**Demand check:** the topic is current (Open Culture, Sept 2026), has an official record page (BnF, UNESCO, Guinness) and the story circulates widely as "Korea before Gutenberg". **Not checked:** search autocomplete and the views of comparable Shorts (no tool for either here); ask the user to look if it matters. **Topic family:** history of technology (printing), new after batteries and metals.

Bridges: 2 *"It is the Jikji…"* (answers the hook), 3 *"Why does that prove anything?"*, 4 *"Scholars found more"*, 5 *"And the book says so itself"*, 6 *"So why…"*, 7 *"So the oldest…"*. **Facts:** five (the upside-down letter and date; block versus pieces; the other anomalies; the postscript; why Gutenberg got the credit and where to read it). **Spoken figures:** three (1377, seventy years, 1972). **Original synthesis:** the 73 to 78 year gap computed from the sources' differing Gutenberg dates, on a number card. **Close:** a concrete payoff that answers the question and tells the viewer something they can do (read it free online), ending on the hook's mistake. **Cut test:** the 39 leaves, Baegun, 1900 Paris, Vever, the 1378 woodblocks, the 1985 dig, UNESCO and Bi Sheng were cut from the voice (some go on cards, the rest into the description). **Scene 1 vs the close:** the hook says "first", scene 7 says "oldest known"; the video never claims more than the sources (BnF, UNESCO). **Weakest claims, attributed or hedged:** scene 4 (summaries only), scene 6 "worked much faster" (one article's judgement).
