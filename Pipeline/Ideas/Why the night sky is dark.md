# Why the night sky is dark

← [[Pipeline]] · [[YouTube Channel]] · [[Idea and hook quality]]

## Idea

**Plain question:** if there are countless stars, why is the night sky dark?

**Theme line (rule 0.9):** *the dark of the night is a clue that the universe had a beginning.*

## Angle

In an endless, unchanging universe every line of sight would end on a star, so the whole sky would blaze (Kepler, 1610; Olbers, 1823). The puzzle ran for centuries. Two things fix it, and **both** are needed: the universe has a **finite age**, so light from the farthest stars has not arrived, and it is **expanding**, which stretches distant starlight into **microwaves at about 2.73 K**, invisible to the eye. **Edgar Allan Poe** guessed the first half in 1848. So the dark between the stars is not empty: it is full of ancient light stretched past visibility.

## Five candidate hooks (plain words, ≤ 10 words)

1. **(proposed)** "A dark night sky tells us the universe began."
2. "In an endless universe, the night sky would blaze."
3. "Edgar Allan Poe solved an astronomy puzzle in 1848."
4. "Astronomers argued for centuries about why night is dark."
5. "The dark between the stars is full of ancient light."

## Skeleton (7 scenes, ≤ 5 facts)

1 the claim (hook) → 2 so why: with endless stars every line ends on a star, the sky should be bright (Kepler, Olbers) → 3 the first half of the answer: the universe has an age, so far light has not arrived (Poe's guess, 1848) → 4 the second half: expansion stretches old light to microwaves, 2.73 K → 5 so the dark is full of light we cannot see → 6 **payoff:** the same darkness you see tonight is the afterglow of the Big Bang, invisible to your eyes.
