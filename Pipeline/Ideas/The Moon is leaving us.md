# The Moon is leaving us

← [[Pipeline]] · [[YouTube Channel]] · [[Idea and hook quality]]

## Idea

**Plain question:** how do we know the Moon is drifting away, and why is it?

**Theme line (rule 0.9):** *the Moon is slowly leaving us, and that will end something you can see today.* (to confirm with the chosen angle)

## Angle (revised 4 Oct 2026)

> **User feedback on the first proposal:** *"first theme is attractive, a mirror left on the moon is boring, core surprise boring."* The theme stays (the Moon is leaving us); **the mirror is dropped as the hook and the core surprise**. It can return as one line of evidence at most. Below are new core surprises; **none is researched yet** (figures are from memory and must be sourced at P1: the agent's search was interrupted by the user, so nothing here is verified).

| # | Core surprise | Plain hook (≤ 10 words) | Why it could be better | Showable | To verify |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A (recommended)** | **Total solar eclipses are a coincidence, and it is ending.** The Moon is about 400 times smaller than the Sun and about 400 times closer, so it covers the Sun almost exactly; because it drifts away, it will one day be too small to cover it | "Perfect solar eclipses are a coincidence, and it's ending." | A beauty people have seen or want to see; a deadline on something they love; scale (Sun, Moon, Earth) | eclipse photos (NASA, Commons) by the dozen: corona, diamond ring, totality, Baily's beads | the 400 × ratio; **how many million years until the last total eclipse (often quoted ≈ 600 million, unchecked)**; that the Moon already covers the Sun only sometimes |
| **B** | **Fossil corals counted the days.** Corals grow daily rings; Devonian fossils show about 400 days in a year, so Earth spun faster and the Moon was closer | "Fossil corals show a year once had 400 days." | The Moon's drift is written in stone; a physical, checkable proof; surprising and plain | coral fossil photos, thin sections, modern coral | the 400-day figure, the age, who measured it (Wells, 1963?) |
| **C** | **The Moon once loomed in the sky.** It formed far closer than today; tides and days were shorter and wilder | "The Moon used to look much bigger." | Visual before and after; stakes for early Earth | size comparisons, NASA concept art (labelled) | how close, how much bigger, day length at formation |

**Proposed thread (A with B as proof):** eclipses are a coincidence of distance; the Moon's drift will end them; fossil corals show the drift is real and Earth is slowing. Facts ≤ 5.

## Five candidate hooks (old set, for the mirror angle; superseded by the table above)

1. "Astronauts left a mirror on the Moon. It's still working." (user: boring)
2. "The Moon is leaving us, and we can measure it."
3. "The Moon drifts 3.8 centimeters farther away every year."
4. "Every day on Earth is a little longer because of the Moon."
5. "We measure the Moon's distance to within a millimeter."

## Skeleton (7 scenes, ≤ 5 facts, each scene bridges to the theme)

1 the mirror (hook) → 2 so what does a mirror do: a laser pulse, one photon in a hundred quadrillion returns, the round trip gives the distance → 3 and the distance is growing: 3.8 cm a year → 4 why: the tides, Earth's bulge pulls the Moon forward and Earth's spin pays → 5 so the day lengthens, 2.4 ms per century → 6 **payoff:** the mirror measures the Moon leaving and Earth slowing in one reading.
