# The Moon is leaving us

← [[Pipeline]] · [[YouTube Channel]] · [[Idea and hook quality]]

## Idea

**Plain question:** how do we know the Moon is drifting away, and why is it?

**Theme line (rule 0.9):** *a mirror left by astronauts shows the Moon is slowly leaving, and Earth's tides are pushing it.*

## Angle

The surprise is the **mirror**: Apollo 11 left a retroreflector in July 1969 and laser pulses from Earth still bounce off it, timed so well that the distance is known to about a millimeter. That timing says the Moon recedes **3.83 cm a year**. The cause is the tides: Earth's spin energy is being passed to the Moon's orbit, so **Earth's days get longer** (2.4 ms per century). The mirror is also a clock for Earth slowing down.

## Facts preview (sources fetched 4 Oct 2026, Wikipedia: *Lunar distance*, *Lunar Laser Ranging experiment*; full bank of ≥ 15 comes at P1)

| Fact | Source |

| Apollo 11 placed the first retroreflector on 21 July 1969; Apollo 14 and 15 added two more (1971) | LLR experiment |

| Distance is measured to millimeter precision (APOLLO, Apache Point: 1.1 mm) | LLR experiment |

## Five candidate hooks (plain words, ≤ 10 words)

1. **(proposed)** "Astronauts left a mirror on the Moon. It's still working."
2. "The Moon is leaving us, and we can measure it."
3. "The Moon drifts 3.8 centimeters farther away every year."
4. "Every day on Earth is a little longer because of the Moon."
5. "We measure the Moon's distance to within a millimeter."

## Skeleton (7 scenes, ≤ 5 facts, each scene bridges to the theme)

1 the mirror (hook) → 2 so what does a mirror do: a laser pulse, one photon in a hundred quadrillion returns, the round trip gives the distance → 3 and the distance is growing: 3.8 cm a year → 4 why: the tides, Earth's bulge pulls the Moon forward and Earth's spin pays → 5 so the day lengthens, 2.4 ms per century → 6 **payoff:** the mirror measures the Moon leaving and Earth slowing in one reading.
