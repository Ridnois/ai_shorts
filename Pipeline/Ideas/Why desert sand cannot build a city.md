# Why desert sand cannot build a city

← [[Pipeline]] · [[YouTube Channel]] · [[Audience]]

**Suggested draft, 6 Oct 2026, not researched.** Topic family: materials and resources. **Y:** building with sand that is free and everywhere. **X:** wind rounds desert grains so they do not lock inside concrete.

## Package (promise paid by scene 1)

- **Promise:** A desert full of sand cannot build a city, because the wind already ruined the sand.
- **Titles:** Why Desert Sand Is Useless for Building (recommended) · Countries Full of Sand Import Sand: Here Is Why · The Shape of a Sand Grain Decides Where Cities Get Built
- **Thumbnail:** hero Sahara dune photo next to angular river sand macro (NASA/Commons, CC); kicker **RESOURCES**; lines **DESERT SAND** / **CAN'T BUILD A CITY** (orange); chips ROUND VS SHARP GRAINS · 50 BILLION T A YEAR · SAND IS RUNNING SHORT. - **Opening map:** scene 1 promise · scene 2 proof and plan.

## Draft script (142 words, words per scene in brackets)

1. The Sahara is mostly sand, yet countries next to deserts import sand to build. The reason is the shape of the grains. (22)
2. Concrete needs sand grains that lock together like rough puzzle pieces. First the shape, then the shortage. (17)
3. Wind rolls desert grains against each other for millions of years, so they end up round and smooth, like tiny marbles. (21)
4. Marbles do not lock. Inside concrete they slide past each other, so the mix comes out weaker. (17)
5. River and sea-floor sand stays sharp and angular, because water carries it only short distances. That is the sand builders dig. (21)
6. The world uses roughly fifty billion tonnes of sand and gravel a year, more than any other solid, so the sharp kind is running short near cities. (27)
7. So a desert full of sand cannot build a city, because the wind already rounded every grain. (17)
