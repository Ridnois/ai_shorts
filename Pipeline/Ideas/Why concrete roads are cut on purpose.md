# Why concrete roads are cut on purpose

## Draft script (161 words, words per scene in brackets)

1. Road crews cut concrete on purpose, and that is what keeps the road from breaking. (15)
2. Concrete shrinks as it dries and cools, but the ground holds it back, so it has to crack. The only choice is where. First the cut, then the crack, then the thump. (32)
3. So crews saw a groove into the fresh slab, about a quarter of its depth. The groove is the weakest line, so the crack forms there, straight and narrow. (29)
4. A crack that forms by chance wanders, lets water in, and in winter that water freezes and pries it wider. (20)
5. Under the groove the two slab edges still touch, rough face against rough face, so they interlock and trucks roll across without a step. (24)
6. Highways add steel dowel bars across the joint to pass the load from one slab to the next. (18)
7. So the thump you hear on a highway is not damage. It is the crack the engineers planned, exactly where they put it. (23)
