# Channel strategy

← [[YouTube Channel]] · [[Shorts quality bar]] · [[Channel identity]] · [[Guidelines for new scripts]]

> **User request (Magnetar feedback, 4 Oct 2026):** *"research youtube monetization and requirements, and design a plan to create a solid, multi platform content channel that is recognizable and solid. I want you to learn how to create a successful youtube shorts channel."* This note holds what was verified, what was not, and the plan. **Decisions that cost money or change the brand are the user's** (§7).

## 1. What YouTube requires to pay (verified 4 Oct 2026, YouTube Help "YouTube Partner Program overview and eligibility")

| Route | Requirement | Where it counts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Shorts route** | **1,000 subscribers and 10 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days** | Shorts public and shown in the Shorts feed |
| **Long-form route** | **1,000 subscribers and 4,000 qualified public watch hours in the last 12 months** | long videos only: **Shorts watch time does not count** |
| Review | about one month after applying | |

- **Not verified:** the lower "early access" tier (fan funding and similar, with lower numbers) is not on the page that was fetched; check YouTube Studio's *Earn* tab before planning around it.
- **Revenue share (Wikipedia, *YouTube Shorts*):** Shorts creators get 45 % of the Shorts ad pool, regular videos 55 %. Per-view earnings of Shorts are small; **the real money path is long videos plus other income** (§5), not the 10-million-view route alone.
- The numbers are a **moving target**: re-read the Help page every quarter and update this note with the date.

## 2. The rule that can kill an AI channel (verified 4 Oct 2026, YouTube Help "YouTube channel monetization policies")

Since **15 July 2025** "repetitious content" is **"inauthentic content"**: it is **not monetizable** when it is *"AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production"*, template videos without creative or educational value, or highly similar storylines repeated. **Allowed:** *series with distinct storylines despite similar formats; a consistent intro or outro while the substance varies.* Reused content needs significant original commentary or educational value.

How this channel stays on the right side (and must keep doing so):

| Risk | What the vault already does | Add |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Template look with swapped words | each video has its own thread, facts bank and scored angle ([[Idea and hook quality]]) | never ship a video whose only difference is the topic word; the **explanatory content and sources are the product** |
| Generic AI visuals | real photos with credits, faithful schematics, callouts naming real parts | keep the ratio ≥ a third stock photos |
| Unoriginal facts | every figure sourced; the angle chosen for surprise | add one **original synthesis** per video (a comparison, a derived number such as "150 GPa ≈ 1.5 million atmospheres", an expert detail) |
| Mass production | the pipeline runs one video at a time with a gate review | **cap cadence** (§4); do not batch twenty videos on one template |

## 3. Disclosure (verified 4 Oct 2026, YouTube Help on altered or synthetic content)

Disclose when content **realistically** depicts something that did not happen or a real person doing or saying something they did not. **Not required** for clearly non-realistic content, and for using AI to *help make* a video (script, edits). This channel's schematics and a synthetic narrator voice are not realistic depictions of real events, so the **YouTube Studio "altered or synthetic" toggle can stay off**, **but the description keeps saying the voice and schematics are AI-generated** (already in the Status template), for honesty and for other platforms' labels. **Never** generate photoreal images of real events or real people. Re-check the policy when it changes.

## 4. Positioning and cadence (proposal)

- **Promise (one line):** *the mechanism behind the headline, in 60 seconds, with the sources on screen.* Narrated learning, curious specialist ([[Channel identity]]). Audience: curious adults who like physics, engineering, materials and how things work (the first 10 videos).
- **A series, not a feed:** 3 recurring formats with the same look and different substance ([[Channel identity]] devices): *The mechanism* (how X works), *The number* (one figure and why it is that), *The catch* (why X is harder than it sounds, like the superconductor freezer). Series names are the user's call.
- **Cadence:** **2 to 3 Shorts a week** is enough to learn and keeps every video reviewed; quality (the [[Shorts quality bar]]) beats volume, and low volume protects against §2. Do not batch.
- **Pair with long videos:** Shorts feed a **monthly 6 to 10 minute video** that goes deeper on the best-performing topic (the only route that earns the 4,000-hour threshold; the Wikipedia summary reports education doing better in long form). A Short ends on the idea; the description links the long video.
- **Topic selection:** the facts-bank score is the filter; add **demand**: search the topic on YouTube and Google Trends before P1 and note the top three existing videos and what they miss.

## 5. Multi-platform plan (proposal; platform rules NOT verified here)

| Layer | Plan |

| **One master** | the 9:16 render with burned-in word captions (already the default), no platform watermark |
| **Platforms** | YouTube Shorts (primary), TikTok, Instagram Reels; later X and a newsletter for sources. Same handle everywhere, same avatar and banner in the copper accent |

| **Pinned comment** | sources and corrections, every video; it builds trust and gives the algorithm comments to read |

## 6. Measure and learn (the loop that makes the channel better)

1. **Per Short, after 48 hours and 7 days**, copy into the video's Status note: views, **viewed vs swiped away** (the 3-second test of rule 0.7), average percentage viewed, subscribers gained, comments. Where to find them: YouTube Studio → Content → the video → Analytics.
2. **After every 10 Shorts**, run [[Feedback loop]] on the *data*, not only on the user's taste: which hooks held, which scenes lost people (the retention graph), which topics gained subscribers; update [[Shorts quality bar]] and [[Idea and hook quality]] with what held.
3. **Milestones (not promises):** 10 videos → first retention data and a hook pattern; 30 videos → judge the series; 1,000 subscribers → apply for the program if the route is within reach; long video #1 after the first 15 Shorts.
4. **Kill criteria:** if 20 Shorts average under about a third of viewers staying past the first seconds, change the *idea* gate and the hook before changing visuals. (The threshold is a guess; replace it with the channel's own median after 10 videos.)
