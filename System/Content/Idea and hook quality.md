# Idea and hook quality

← [[YouTube Channel]] · [[Guidelines for new scripts]] · [[Narration guide]] · [[Production process]]

> **Why this note exists.** Feedback on [[Helium - why it cannot be made - Status]]: the hook was boring ("looks for a reaction before there is a reason to react"), and the ideas themselves were weak: *"of a universe of bright stars, you pointed at a street lamp. If the ideas around the script aren't good enough, the process will not push quality and value forward."* Polish, budget and editing cannot rescue a weak idea, so **idea quality is now a gate before any scripting** (P1), applied by default in every run, delegated or not.

## 1. Map the sky before choosing a star (stage 0–1)

Before writing a script, write a **facts bank** at the top of the Research note: **at least 15 remarkable true things about the topic**, looking for range, not for the first useful facts:

| Look at the topic from… | Ask |
| --- | --- |
| **Origin and scale** | Where did it come from, in the universe, in the Earth, in history? How big or old is it? |
| **Strange behaviour** | What does it do that breaks intuition? What is the paradox? |
| **Extremes** | The coldest, fastest, rarest, oldest, most abundant… |
| **Connections** | What bigger thing is it secretly part of (stars, life, civilisation, other fields)? |
| **Open mysteries and expert detail** | What do specialists find the most beautiful part? What does a casual explainer always skip? |
| **Use and consequence** | Only after the above: what does it do for us, who depends on it? (This is the street lamp: it comes last, not first.) |

Every fact gets a source and a date as usual ([[Guidelines for new scripts]] §1, Rigor). Facts that are only "true and relevant" do not make the bank; they must be **remarkable**.

## 2. Score and choose

**Read `Analytics/Report.md` first** ([[Analytics]] §4): once videos have data, a hook type or topic family that held `viewed_pct`, `shares` and `subs_gained` above the channel's median earns a bonus when ranking angles; one that fell below is not repeated without a new angle. With fewer than 10 videos with data the report guides, it does not decide.

Score each fact or angle 1 to 5 (a table in the Research note, shown at P1):

| Criterion | 5 means |
| --- | --- |
| **Surprise** | A smart non-expert has probably never heard it |
| **Scale** | It connects to something much bigger than the object (a star, a law of physics, a civilisation) |
| **Depth** | It has a *why* that rewards understanding, not just a number |
| **Evidence** | Two sources, or one primary source |
| **Showable** | A real object with a photo can carry it |
| **Plain** | Someone with no background can say what it is after one sentence, with no defined term (new, Superconductors feedback: *"not surprising nor clear enough"*; the agent had scored its own surprise 5) |

**Rules:** the thread of the video must score **≥ 4 on surprise, on scale and on plain**. **Scores are the agent's guess: run the coffee test** (say the thread in one plain sentence as if to a friend; if the friend would say "so what?", it scores ≤ 3 on surprise). Keep the top one as the thread and two or three as supports. Write the rejected top candidates in one line each, so the user can swap.

**Street-lamp test.** If the central question could be answered by a news headline or a product spec ("MRI machines use a lot of helium"), it is a street lamp. Go back to §1.

**Expert test.** Would a specialist read the script and say "yes, that is the interesting part", not "that is the textbook summary"? Each scene carries **one detail a generic explainer would miss**.

## 3. The hook gives a reason before it asks for a reaction

- **The hook is the most astonishing true thing about the subject, stated plainly, with its meaning attached.** The reaction comes from the idea, never from the delivery.
- **Plain words (Superconductors feedback, hook rated 2 of 5):** no jargon the viewer needs defined ("zero resistance"); say the effect in everyday words and let the jargon arrive in scene 2.
- **A big number alone is not a hook.** "Fifteen hundred liters" means nothing until the viewer knows why it matters.
- Prefer **universal scale, paradox, or behaviour that breaks intuition**. Sound like someone who knows the field: specific, confident, no filler.
- **Write five candidate hooks** in the Research note and pick one with two tests: *would I stop if a friend told me this?* and *does it open a question the next scene answers?* A hook that is only a reaction ("you won't believe…") fails.
- **The first scene is a need-to-know claim** (Magnetar feedback, 4 Oct 2026: *"you could start with a phrase to start curiosity… something you need to know after 3 seconds"*): ≤ 10 words, its first clause spoken inside 3 seconds, a true statement that makes the next sentence necessary. A scene-setting opener (a date, a place, "imagine") is a story, not a claim: move it to scene 2 as evidence. Craft: [[Narration guide]] §2, [[Shorts quality bar]] §1.

## 4. Worked example: helium (facts to verify with sources before use)

The video that triggered this note opened on the MRI's 1,500 liters, a street lamp. The sky was wider: helium is the **second most abundant element in the universe**, made in the first minutes after the Big Bang and in the cores of stars; on Earth it also forms underground by radioactive decay, yet almost none of it stays on the planet. It is **inert, it cannot burn or explode**; it **does not freeze at normal pressure even at absolute zero**; below ≈ 2.17 K it becomes a **superfluid** that flows without friction and climbs the walls of its container. A hook in that spirit: *"Helium is the second most common thing in the universe, and almost none of it stays on Earth."* Only then does the MRI, the supply and the sealed magnets become **consequences** of a remarkable thing, not the story itself. (These facts were not researched in this vault yet: they go through §1 with sources first.)
