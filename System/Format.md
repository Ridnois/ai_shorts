# Format: what a Next Token Short is

How it looks: [[Look]]. How it is made: [[Production]]. Before the click and after the upload: [[Growth]].

**One sentence:** a 45 to 60 s vertical story that shows one real AI thing happening, explains the hidden mechanism in plain words, and ends on something the viewer can use or repeat. English, five a week, Mon to Fri.

## 1. Series (each video belongs to one; two in a row never share one)

| Series | Promise | Example title |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **How AI works** | the mechanism behind something people see AI do | "Why ChatGPT can't count the r's in strawberry" |
| **Agent at work** | a real recorded run of an AI tool doing a task, and the one trick that made it work | "I let Claude Code fix a bug while I watched" |
| **What's coming** | a change with evidence: compute, energy, jobs, rules | "Why AI companies are buying nuclear plants" |

## 2. The idea passes 4 tests (5 minutes, not an hour)

1. **Real thing:** a named product, company, person or incident, dated. No real thing, no video.
2. **Curiosity gap:** a stranger who uses ChatGPT says "wait, why?" to the one-line claim. Say it aloud; "so?" is a fail.
3. **Demand:** 2 or more Shorts or videos on the same question did 3x or more their channel's median (`youtube_search.py`, [[Growth]] §Ideas), or the plain question autocompletes.
4. **Showable:** the mechanism can be drawn or recorded, not only said.

Record the claim, the real thing, the demand evidence and 3 hooks in the Status note. Nothing else is required.

## 3. The script: 130 to 170 words (45 to 60 s at rate 1.1)

| Beat | Time | Job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Hook** | 0 to 3 s | the strangest true fact, in 12 words or fewer, on screen as text and picture at frame 0 |
| **Stake** | 3 to 10 s | why it matters to the viewer: a cost, a failure, a surprise with a name and a number |
| **Mechanism** | 10 to 35 s | how it works, on the one running example, in 2 or 3 steps; each step changes the picture |
| **Turn** | about 60 % | a "but" that flips what the viewer just assumed; the best fact goes here |
| **Payoff** | last 10 s | what to do or say with it, then a last line that runs back into the first (the loop) |

- **Hook rules:** visual, spoken and on-screen hook name the same thing (the three-hook rule). Name the subject the viewer knows ("ChatGPT", not "a model"). No "Did you know", no "Imagine", no date opener, no question opener. The keyword the viewer would search is spoken in the first sentence (TikTok and YouTube read the transcript).
- **Open loops:** the hook opens a question that the mechanism answers; the turn opens a second one that the payoff closes. A re-hook (a new question, number or reversal) every 8 to 12 s.
- **Worked case first:** a mechanism the viewer has never met is shown working on a case small enough to count on screen (3 switches: all 8 settings tried, the passing ones lit), with every part named as it appears (what a switch is, what a rule is, when a rule holds); only then is it scaled up. Naming a puzzle is not showing it (user, 30 Oct 2026: "I dont get it yet, could you be more explanatory on the switches part?").
- **For the ear:** sentences of 6 to 16 words, one idea each, joined by "because", "so", "but". Numbers and acronyms written as spoken ("G P U"). No exclamation marks.
- **The loop ending:** read the last sentence into the first; if it plays as one sentence, it passes. No "like and subscribe". The closing question, if any, is one the viewer can answer in one word.
- **Cut test:** delete each sentence in turn; if the video still works, it stays deleted.
- **Rigor:** every number has a source and date in the Script note; every AI claim names model, version and date; the narrator describes mechanisms, never wants ("it predicts the next word", not "it thinks").

## 4. The picture (the quality bar)

| Rule | Test |
| --- | --- |
| **Frame 0 is the thumbnail** | the hook's picture and its claim text are on screen at 0 s, readable at 170 px wide |
| **Show the verb** | every beat: "the viewer sees ___ happen, so they understand ___"; a picture any other video could use is cut |
| **The subject fills the frame** | the hero spans 70 to 90 % of the width; no area over a third of the frame stays empty for 2 s |
| **An event every 1.5 to 2 s** | a part moves, a number counts, the camera pushes, a word lands; nothing holds still over 3 s, and nothing over 1.5 s while the hook plays (`pace.py` measures it) |
| **Real over drawn** | a real product is shown as itself (screen capture, recorded run, real page); a machine is drawn as an [[Look]] Anatomy or Hairline figure; never a fake UI |
| **Three shot sizes** | wide, medium and close inside the same world, through the camera |
| **No AI clichés** | no glowing brains, robots, circuit faces, binary rain, handshakes |
| **Varied from the last video** | a new main figure or capture; the inauthentic-content policy punishes "interchangeable" videos ([[Growth]] §Policy) |

## 5. Sound

One continuous voice take ([[Production]] §Voice). Effects only on a visible action (whoosh on a camera move, click on a callout, pop on a chip, one hit on the payoff). A quiet music bed is allowed when licensed and ducked under the voice. Master at −14 LUFS, true peak −1 dBTP.

## 6. What "done" means

A render the agent has audited ([[Production]] §Checks) plus the publish pack. The user's verdict is **ship**, **fix** (one list, one revision) or **drop**; at most two revisions. A Short that waits more than 7 days is shipped or dropped: uploading beats polishing, because only uploads produce data ([[Growth]] §Analytics).
