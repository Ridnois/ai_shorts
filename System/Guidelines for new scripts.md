# Guidelines for new scripts

← [[YouTube Channel]] · [[Fast workflow]] · [[Shorts quality bar]] · [[Channel strategy]] · [[Channel playbook]]

**Read it whole before writing a script.** It is the feedback from the videos ([[Magnets and geopolitics - Status]], [[Helium and power - Status]], [[Brazil and embassies - Status]], [[Lemon cake - Status]], [[Magnetar - Status]], [[Superconductors - Status]]) reduced to rules. Each rule says where it comes from. Production details (models, prices, budget) are in [[Fast workflow]].

> **Language (user decision, 3 Oct 2026): every new script is written and narrated in English**, with English on-screen text, English prompts and an English description. Research, status notes and budgets are also in English. The earlier videos (Spanish narration) are a record and are not redone unless the user asks. Voice: [[Channel voice]].

> **What good content is (user's definition):** the kind you can enjoy and keep watching *even knowing it is AI*; informative or absurd, but it delivers its message. Channel goal: **narrated learning**, educate and entertain. References: *Sleep on Physics*, *Sleep on Science*. Detail: [[YouTube with AI - quality content guide]].

## 0. Idea and hook (read before anything else)

Source: post-render feedback on the first English helium video (4 Oct 2026): *"the hook is boring… looks for a reaction before there is a reason to react to it"* and *"of a universe of bright stars, you pointed at a street lamp"*. Full method: [[Idea and hook quality]].

| # | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| 0.1 | **Map the sky first.** The Research note opens with a **facts bank of ≥ 15 remarkable true things** about the topic (origin and scale, strange behaviour, extremes, connections, expert details); uses and news come last. |
| 0.2 | **Score and choose.** Surprise, scale, depth, evidence, showable (1 to 5). **The thread must score ≥ 4 on surprise and on scale.** Show the table and the one-line rejected candidates at P1. |
| 0.3 | **Street-lamp test.** If a news headline or a product spec could answer the central question, it is a street lamp: go back to 0.1. |
| 0.4 | **Expert test.** A specialist would call it "the interesting part", not "the textbook summary"; each scene carries one detail a generic explainer would skip. |
| 0.5 | **The hook gives a reason before it asks for a reaction:** the most astonishing true thing, stated plainly, with its meaning attached. A big number alone is not a hook. Write **five candidate hooks**; pick by "would I stop?" and "does it open a question the next scene answers?". |
| 0.6 | **Plan by words, not by time** (see *Duration and pace*). |
| 0.8 | **Plain-words test.** A viewer with no background must understand the first sentence **without any term defined later**. "Zero resistance" failed it (Superconductors feedback, 4 Oct 2026: *"it vague, I may not know what zero resistance means"*, hook rated 2 of 5). Say the effect in everyday words ("Some wires waste no electricity. Almost nobody can use them."), and introduce the jargon word in scene 2, after the viewer cares. |
| 0.9 | **One thread, a fact budget.** The video has one **theme line** (the question in plain words, e.g. *"a wire that wastes nothing, and why we cannot use it yet"*). It is stated in scene 1 or 2, **every scene opens with a bridge back to it** ("so", "that is why", "and this is the catch") and the close answers it. **At most 5 facts and 3 spoken figures per Short;** a fact that does not move the theme is cut however good it is. Feedback: *"I got lost on the facts and forgot the theme of the video."* |
| 0.7 | **The first 3 seconds carry a need-to-know claim, not a story.** Scene 1 is ≤ 10 words and its first clause (≈ 7 words) lands inside 3 s: a true, specific claim that makes the viewer need the next sentence ("Some wires carry electricity with zero loss."). **No scene-setting opener** ("In May 2025, an assembly line…", "Imagine…"). The whole video **explains, it does not narrate**: facts and mechanisms in cause → effect order, no storytelling voice. Source: Magnetar feedback, 4 Oct 2026. Craft: [[Narration guide]] §2 and [[Shorts quality bar]] §1. |

## 1. Script

**Craft and structure of a Short: [[Narration guide]]** (source of truth). This table is the feedback that originated it.

| # | Rule | Comes from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1.1 | **Tension comes from context, not from waiting.** A concrete fact that shows what is at stake builds more tension than a pause. | Magnets: "slow, as if forcing curiosity" |
| 1.2 | **Every hook is resolved within its scene or the next**, and answers all three: **what it is**, **why it happened**, **what it means**. | Magnets: "was a magnet missing? why? what does it mean?" |
| 1.3 | **Simplifying is not removing the why.** If you must cut, sacrifice the side detail (dates, names, secondary figures), not the cause → effect chain. | Magnets |
| 1.4 | **Make them think.** Deliver an idea the viewer closes alone (e.g. "a case that falls on procedure never gets to judge the facts"; "if it goes wrong, it is not the recipe: it is a step"). A list of facts is not enough. | Brazil (feedback 2) |
| 1.5 | **Focus on causes, not lists.** Every step or case carries its why in the same sentence. | Lemon cake |
| 1.6 | **Brisk pace:** short sentences full of information, no filler pauses. Silence is used **once, on purpose** (e.g. 1.2 s before the central question). | Magnets, Brazil |
| 1.7 | **One new idea per scene.** If a scene explains two things, split it. | Magnets |
| 1.8 | **Write for the ear and read it aloud.** Whatever sounds forced or dramatic gets rewritten. | Magnets |
| 1.9 | **Figures and dates go on screen, not in the voice** (`180 °C`, `27 years · 4 to 1`). | Brazil, Lemon cake |
| 1.10 | **The ending is a concrete payoff, not an aphorism.** One plain sentence that answers the video's question **and** says what it means for a person or a thing they know ("So the first superconducting power line to leave the freezer will make every other line obsolete"). "We already own the miracle" rated 1 of 5: it sounds wise and says nothing. Test: *could the viewer repeat it to a friend and be understood?* | Superconductors (*"vague ending as the rest of the script"*) |
| 1.11 | **Cut test (compact script).** After the draft, delete each sentence in turn; if the video still answers its question and keeps its through-line, the sentence stays deleted. Then re-count the facts (§0.9). | Superconductors (*"boring, feels unconnected, could be more compacted"*) |
| 1.12 | **Engaging is stakes plus specificity, not volume.** Every fact is followed by *what it changes* ("so a line that wastes nothing…"), at least in two scenes; sentences vary in length; at most one rhetorical question; no exclamation marks. A **delivery note per scene** (calm, rising, amused, grave) is written in the script table for the voice ([[Channel voice]]). | Superconductors (*"make the content more engaging and interesting"*, voice *"more controlled and passionate"*) |

### Rigor

- **Every figure with source and date**; weak material stays out of the script or is attributed ("according to…"). Use "can" / "could" when the source is weak. Check the premise of the request: Brazil *warned it could* close embassies, it did not close them.
- **Convicted ≠ accused ≠ investigated ≠ dismissed.** Use the exact words of the ruling; same treatment for every character; nobody gets adjectives.
- **If sources contradict each other** (recipe quantities), publish it as "a base recipe from several sources" and say so in the description.
- **Focus of the request:** if the user fixes a focus ("candidates, their crimes and how the election is sabotaged"), respect it; do not widen it.

### Duration and pace

- **Words are the unit; duration is a result** (user feedback, 4 Oct 2026: *"give it a word range instead of a time range"*). A Short is planned as **130 to 190 words, aiming at 130 to 160** (Superconductors feedback: *"could be more compacted"*; more than 160 only when every scene passes the cut test, §1.11), in 6 to 8 scenes (per-scene ranges in [[Narration guide]] §2). **Do not write a target time in the notes before the voice exists**; the length appears afterwards, from the generated voice, and is reported as *derived* (≈ words × 0.42 s at the measured 144 wpm; render names carry the measured duration). Short is fine; longer is fine if the words earn it. Do not speed up the voice to make it fit.
- **Adapt the script to the pace of the voice, or use a model with `speed`**; do not cut blindly or regenerate. Planning: ≈ 145 to 150 words per minute with `elevenlabs/v3` at `speed` 0.95 (Lemon cake, Spanish: 197 words → 77.6 s; Chips, Spanish: 211 words → 88 s; **Helium, English: 175 words → 72.9 s ≈ 144 wpm**). **Measure with the complete script**, not one sentence (a single sentence gave 122; the whole script, 156). Details in [[Channel voice]].
- Hook in the **first scene**, with the strongest image, per rules 0.5 and 0.7.

### Checklist before P3

- [ ] Is there a facts bank of ≥ 15, a scoring table, and a thread with surprise ≥ 4 and scale ≥ 4? (rules 0.1–0.2)
- [ ] Does it pass the street-lamp test and the expert test? (0.3–0.4)
- [ ] Were five hooks written, and does the chosen one give a reason before asking for a reaction? (0.5)
- [ ] Is the length stated in words, inside 130 to 190? (0.6)
- [ ] Does scene 1 make a need-to-know claim in ≤ 10 words, first clause inside 3 s, with no scene-setting? (0.7)
- [ ] Plain-words test: would someone with no background understand the first sentence with no defined term? (0.8)
- [ ] Can the theme line be said in one sentence, and does every scene bridge back to it? Facts ≤ 5, spoken figures ≤ 3? (0.9)
- [ ] Cut test passed (1.11) and the ending is a concrete payoff (1.10)?
- [ ] Does every hook answer what it is, why and what it means?
- [ ] Is there a pause or sentence that exists only to create suspense?
- [ ] Does someone who knows nothing understand the cause → effect chain **without seeing the images**?
- [ ] Does it leave an idea that makes you think, without reproach or sermon?
- [ ] Read aloud: where does it drag?
- [ ] Is every claim supported or attributed?

## 2. Illustrations (full method: [[Illustration pipeline]])

Source feedback: **Magnetar, 4 Oct 2026** (*"illustrations are too creative and unscientific; blue empty background with added text is strange, illustrations could go there"*), Brazil ("no relation between the illustrations and the content"), Lemon cake ("horrible illustration… look up a homemade lemon cake"), and **Twisted carbon, 4 Oct 2026**: *"the images feel not to be representing the content, nor describing the scenes… less approach on scene description, it is not a theatre show, it is modern content… use illustrations where they are useful, describe machines and explain processes… use free stock photos… use the model on the style it performs best, not a custom illustration style… transparent backgrounds with gradients applied in Blender."*

| # | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| 2.1 | **Every plate passes the usefulness test:** one sentence, "this plate lets the viewer see ___ so they understand ___". If it cannot be written, replace or cut the plate. |
| 2.2 | **Write the visual plan before any prompt** (stage 3): one row per plate with its **type** (stock photo, generated explainer, cutout, photo edit, number plate), its source and its usefulness sentence. **A new image every ≈ 3 to 4 s and none held longer than 5 s** (user feedback, 4 Oct 2026: *"add more reference images, so it changes faster"*): about **16 to 20 plates for a 160-word Short**. Default mix: **at least half real photos or real scientific figures (free), ≥ a third explainers or cutouts, no two consecutive plates of the same type, unless both the treatment (`full`, `contain`, `cutout`) and the subject differ** (Superconductors: a `full` pylon photo, then the hook photo again as a `contain` plate on a gradient; with ≤ 5 generated images and honest photos only, strict alternation was impossible). |
| 2.3 | **Real things are photographed, hidden things are explained.** Real subjects → free stock photos (Openverse, NASA, Wikimedia; credits recorded). Insides, processes, structures and scales → a generated explainer (cutaway, cross-section, exploded view, schematic). Never a decorative stand-in. |
| 2.4 | **Prompts are specifications, not scenes:** exact object or process, its parts and their count and arrangement, the view, what must be readable, what each reference supplies. No mood, no "dramatic", no theatre, no props that explain nothing. ([[Prompt - per-scene generation guide]]) |
| 2.5 | **No custom style.** No style block, no clay, no sketch. The model's default look is the style; the look of the video comes from the Blender edit (gradients, shadows, framing, callouts, text). |
| 2.6 | **Generate full frames or transparent cutouts** (`gpt-image-2`, `background: transparent`); place them on gradients in Blender (`type: full | contain | cutout` in `plan.json`). |
| 2.7 | **Check every plate against the facts** (counts, structure, labels) and for leaked text. A beautiful but wrong cutaway fails 2.1. One retry per image; if it fails twice, change the prompt's content or the plate type. |
| 2.8 | **Vertical 9:16, safe zone:** subject between 15 % and 60 % of the height; the card zone (≈ 21 % to 36 % from the bottom) stays free; callout labels never sit over the card. |
| 2.9 | **Plates without the voice must still make sense.** If the images are not understood without the narration, the plan is not ready. |
| 2.10 | **Check every reference and stock photo for text and brands** before using it; a legible brand leaks into generated images and distracts in photos. Prefer references without lettering. |
| 2.11 | **Do not paste the narration sentence into an image prompt.** In the Helium video one prompt that began with `Narration: "…"` printed that sentence, garbled, inside the image. Paraphrase the idea, add "The image contains no letters, no numbers and no labels", and check every plate for text. |
| 2.13 | **Scientific fidelity beats creativity.** An explainer shows only what the research note supports: real structure, real counts, real relative scale. **Anything decorative that looks like data is wrong** (the Magnetar plate's glowing arcs read as field lines but were invented). Prompt it as a *textbook or agency schematic* of the established model; give it a real diagram or photo as reference; list in the prompt what must **not** be invented ("no glow, no energy rays, no decorative particles"). After generation, **check each element against the research note**; if an element has no source, regenerate once without it or replace the plate with a photo. |
| 2.15 | **Reference images are the main source of change.** Find **2 to 3 candidate photos or figures per scene** before the visual plan (Commons, NASA, Openverse; real diagrams from papers and Wikipedia are welcome as `contain` plates with credit). The same photo may appear twice **if the crop shows a different detail** (`focus`, `zoom`). Realism: when a real photo exists, **photo plus callouts beats a generated schematic**; a generated plate is for what cannot be photographed, built from a real reference with `input_fidelity: high` and a *realistic* render of the real device, not an abstract diagram of spheres, and it is labelled "schematic" in the Status note when simplified (Superconductors feedback: *"be more realistic and related with…"*, illustrations rated 3). |
| 2.14 | **No bare backdrops.** A number card, quote or callout never sits on an empty gradient. It goes **on a real photo or a faithful explainer of the thing it measures** (a number card about a field strength sits on the magnet, not on violet). `build_video.py` refuses blank placeholders. A gradient is only the surface behind a cutout or a `contain` photo. |
| 2.12 | **Record every credit** (title, author, license, page) in `stock/_credits.jsonl` as the photo is downloaded; the Status note's attribution table is built from it. |

### Text and added content (full rules: [[On-screen text style]], [[Channel identity]])

| # | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| T.1 | Text has a purpose: **name a part** (callout), **quantify** (number card), **frame and source** (context card), **give a voice** (margin note, ≤ 2 per video). |
| T.2 | **The specificity test:** could this text appear unchanged in a video about another topic? If yes, rewrite it with a name, number, place or mechanism from this topic. |
| T.3 | **Banned:** generic labels ("The takeaway", "Key point", "Same material") and the banned words in [[Channel identity]]. |
| T.4 | Not every plate needs a card; a plate with callouts or a number does not also need a context card. |

## 3. Voice and sound

- **Voice: `elevenlabs/v3`, `Austin`, `language_code: en`** (user, 5 Oct 2026; replaces `Roger`), `speed` as set in [[Channel voice]] (1.0 for the first Austin video), `previous_text` / `next_text` for intonation. "Elevenlabs is better." (`qwen3-tts` only as a cheap alternative, no speed control.) **I cannot hear the voice: it is the user's choice**, and it is not changed afterwards. Details: [[Channel voice]], test: [[Voice samples]].
- **One take per scene, with the exact script text.** Sentences are never cut (see §4).
- **The channel's sound design is synthesized by the builder and is on by default** (user request, 4 Oct 2026: *"add sound design"*, after the Magnetar render; it replaces "no sound by default"): `plan.json` `sound` → `build_video.py` writes an original WAV with numpy (no model, no license, no cost): a short sub thump on the first frame, a soft whoosh on each scene cut, a quieter tick on each plate change inside a scene, a two-partial ping when a card arrives, a tiny click when a callout arrives, and a very quiet A-minor drone bed. Levels, measured on the Superconductors video: voice ≈ −22 dBFS while speaking, effects ≈ −31 dBFS, bed ≈ −37 dBFS; `sfx_gain` and `bed_gain` in the plan adjust them. **The agent cannot hear it**: it is checked by numbers and declared at P7 ([[Blender editing]] §Sound design). **For anything else (a real recording, a music track), sound and music are opt-in, from free licensed libraries, never invented** (research of 4 Oct 2026, [[Research - Free resource directories (FMHY)]]). Before the sound design existed the default was none: the user rejected the first generated sound ("no blender sound") and Brazil's feedback asked for better quality and dynamism, so a script may propose sound **only as a row in the visual plan (P4a) with a source, a license and a purpose**, and it is used only after approval.
  - **Sources, in order:** YouTube Audio Library (music and effects; no Content ID claims; YouTube only) → Sonniss GDC bundles (effects; no credit; no AI/ML use) → Pixabay (check each track) → Incompetech (music; CC BY 4.0, credit line mandatory). **BigSoundBank and Freesound are deferred**: not used and no helper built until the user says so.
  - **Never:** BBC Sound Effects (RemArc, non-commercial), any CC BY-NC file, game-rip sites, and any library whose license was not read (NCS, BenSound, Free Music Archive).
  - **Every sound is credited:** `Media/<video>/sound/_credits.jsonl` (title, author, license, page) copied to the Status attribution table; CC BY goes into the description.
  - **Use it as a cue, not as a claim:** under the voice (volume 0.18 to 0.22, 0.3 s in, 0.4 s out), music bed ≤ 0.12, one repeated tick for number cards, silence as a tool; a sound never implies a real-world sound it is not.
  - `audiogen` (16 kHz) and other generators are a fallback only with approval; `mmaudio` does not work with text only; `stable-audio-2.5` is out because of price.

## 4. Editing

- **A voice sentence is never cut, and never starts abruptly** (Brazil r1 error; Twisted carbon feedback, 4 Oct 2026: *"small voice cut on the start of the video, make sure voice clips are complete and well put into the sequencer"*). The TTS files often start mid-sound (some at full amplitude at sample 0) and end abruptly. `build_video.py` therefore **pads every take** (0.30 s of silence before, 0.40 s after, a 20 ms fade-in and a 50 ms fade-out, as `voice/_padded/*.wav`), places it so that the speech plus ≥ 0.1 s before and ≥ 0.25 s after are audible, and **checks every voice** (`CHECK=1`): lead-in, tail, silence in the padded file, no overlaps. Blender's background mixdown is silent, so the check reads the strips directly.
- **Silences:** 0.45 s between scenes; 1.2 s before the central question; **≤ 0.2 s of intro** (`start` in the plan: speech starts at once, [[Shorts quality bar]] §1) and 1.2 s of tail.
- **On-screen text:** callouts, number cards, context cards and margin notes ([[On-screen text style]]), composed from `plan.json`.
- **Word-by-word captions on every video** (Magnetar feedback, 4 Oct 2026: *"add transcriptions to the video, word by word, so users can follow the narrative with the audio off"*). `plan.json` carries `texts` (the narration of each scene, exactly as voiced); **`captions` (optional) carries the display spelling when it differs from the voiced one** ("250 K", "LK-99", "1911"); `System/scripts/word_timings.py` times the words from the voice files (whisper, ≈ $0.01 per take) and `build_video.py` shows **one big word at a time** (white, dark outline, figures in copper) in the caption zone in the middle of the frame (50 % of the height; moved from 16 % from the bottom on 5 Oct 2026 because the Shorts interface covered it). Without timings the builder estimates them. Cards and callouts never enter the caption zone ([[On-screen text style]] §3).
- **Plates:** one every ≈ 3 to 4 s (16 to 20 plates for 160 words; the builder prints `LONG PLATE` for any plate over 5 s), cross-fade within the scene and "page turn" between scenes, an 8 % push that alternates in and out; types `full`, `contain` and `cutout` over gradients ([[Illustration pipeline]] §6). Plate changes should land on the word they explain (not yet synced to each word).
- **Say what was not verified:** the full video is not watched or heard, only frames and headers; gate P7 is the user's.
- **Before putting a symbol on screen** (→, ≈, ≥, ≤), check that the typeface has it: Noto Sans Display Bold has none of them and they show as boxes ("→" in Stems r1, "≈" again in Battery and lithium r1, 5 Oct 2026, after this very warning). **Never write ≈ ≥ ≤ → in a card or callout:** use "about", "or more", "to" or "·". Also keep card captions under about 40 characters: a longer one is cut off at the card's edge.
- Details: [[Blender editing]], [[Transitions and effects]].

## 5. Process, costs and files

- **Master rule: no media is generated before P4** (prompts + budget approved, in dollars). Every approval is written in the video's Status. If the user **delegates** ("carry on until the render"), note it and run without stopping, but respect the cap.
- **Cost:** it worries the user ("it is getting expensive, too much testing"). Cap per category = base × 1.2; a minimal test at P5; series of ≤ 10 calls, **in series**, never in parallel; fix the prompt before retrying. Going over the cap needs approval.
- **The images that are generated are the ones in the cut**, 1 per plate by default; a Short plans **≤ 3 generated images (stock first; from 5 Oct 2026 with `gpt-image-2`)** and the rest are free stock photos or built in Blender.
- **Research goes separate from scripts** (models, styles, references): notes in `Research/`, tests in `Media/_canal/investigacion/`, cost accounted separately. A video note only links the conclusion.
- **Do not move other videos' folders.** Files carry their video's prefix (Obsidian resolves embeds by name). Discards go to `_descartes/`.
- **Notes in the vault, never artifacts or Claude Docs.**
- **Copy-paste text (user, 5 Oct 2026: *"make sure description is easy to copy and paste, not \">\" or not allowed characters"*):** every text the user pastes into a platform (description, caption, pinned comment) sits in a ```` ```text ```` block (one copy button, no `>` quote marks), in plain ASCII: `-` for bullets, `|` as a separator, no `•`, `·`, curly quotes or `<` `>` (YouTube rejects angle brackets). The TikTok caption and pinned comment follow the same rule.
- **Upload package (updated 5 Oct 2026):** every video gets `Upload - <Topic>.md` in its folder, built from [[Upload note template]] (the video embedded, three titles, the description, five relevant hashtags, thumbnail candidates, pinned comment, Studio settings, **a TikTok section** ([[TikTok upload]]), the research behind each choice, and a pointer to the metrics: they live in `Analytics/`, not in the note). **Cadence: one Short a day** (user, 5 Oct 2026); the description says "New Shorts every day." **Measure:** at P8 fill the video's row in `Analytics/videos.csv`; run `youtube_analytics.py pull` at about 24 h, 7 d and 28 d; **before choosing the next idea and hook read `Analytics/Report.md`** ([[Analytics]]). **Thumbnail:** custom Shorts thumbnails exist since 24 July 2026 for Partner Program channels (desktop); otherwise choose the opening frame; the feed shows none (see [[Fast workflow]] §4 rule 9). **Description:** the claim and search words in the first two lines, sources as real links, photo credits for CC BY and CC BY-SA, cut-off date, the AI disclosure, 3 to 5 relevant hashtags last. Everything in English.

### Run-time lessons (Twisted carbon, 4 Oct 2026)

- **Wikimedia:** the thumbnail/API endpoint throttles (429, timeouts). Fetch originals directly: `https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/<md5[0]>/<md5[:2]>/<File_name>` (md5 of the file name with underscores), resize locally, and read author and license with one small API call.
- **Replicate helper:** `System/scripts/replicate_helper.py` has timeouts and download retries, prints the prediction id at creation and has `recover(id, dest)`; use it if a download hangs, never regenerate.
- **Reading prompts from the Status note:** Obsidian re-pads table columns on save, so parsers must tolerate spaces around the pipes.
- **Never `pkill -f` a pattern that also appears in the shell command** (it kills the shell); match the process name and arguments with `ps` and `awk`.
- **Order plates by the sentence they illustrate** (Asteroid mining build, 5 Oct 2026): the builder splits a scene's time equally among its plates, so the third plate is on screen while the last third of the sentence is spoken; put the photo of the Moon last if the voice says "Moon" last. Look at stills at the *spoken* word, not at the plate start.
- **Image models restyle diagrams into abstractions** (a moiré diagram became clay swirls): use diagrams as *references for a generated explainer* or show them as `contain` plates, not as sources to be redrawn.
