# Story: the words of a Short

Which idea and which hook pattern: [[Strategy]]. What the viewer sees: [[Picture]]. Where viewers left before: [[Data]] §Review.

**One sentence:** a 45 to 60 s vertical story that shows one real AI thing happening, explains the hidden mechanism in plain words, and ends on something the viewer can use or repeat.

## Beats: 130 to 170 words (45 to 60 s at rate 1.1)

| Beat | Time | Job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Hook** | 0 to 3 s | the strangest true fact, in 12 words or fewer, in one pattern of [[Strategy]] §Hooks; on screen as text and picture at frame 0 |
| **Stake** | 3 to 10 s | why it matters to the viewer: what it cost, broke or changed, with a number. **It raises the stakes; it does not cite them** |
| **Mechanism** | 10 to 35 s | how it works, on the one running example, in 2 or 3 steps; each step changes the picture |
| **Turn** | about 60 % | a "but" that flips what the viewer just assumed; the best fact goes here |
| **Payoff** | last 10 s | what to do or say with it, then a last line that runs back into the first (the loop) |

- **The 4 to 7 s rule** (Data review, 3 Nov 2026: 12 of 14 curves fell hardest there, on lines like "was July 2025. Founder" and "a 2019 paper, Frankle and Carbin"): the second and third sentences carry consequence and a fresh question, never a date, a paper, an author, a trial size or an institution. The source appears **on screen** (a tag, the real page) while the voice moves on; the voice may name it after 10 s if the name helps.
- **Hook rules:** visual, spoken and on-screen hook name the same thing (the three-hook rule). Name the subject the viewer knows ("ChatGPT", not "a model"). No "Did you know", no "Imagine", no date opener, no question opener. The search keyword is spoken in the first sentence (YouTube and TikTok read the transcript).
- **Promise, proof, plan** ([[User insights]]): by 10 s the viewer knows they clicked the right video (the promise restated as a stake), has a reason to trust it (the real thing on screen), and knows where it is going (the question the mechanism will answer).
- **Open loops:** the hook opens a question that the mechanism answers; the turn opens a second one that the payoff closes. A re-hook (a new question, number or reversal) every 8 to 12 s, on a scene change ([[Picture]] §Pace).
- **Worked case first:** a mechanism the viewer has never met is shown working on a case small enough to count on screen (3 switches: all 8 settings tried, the passing ones lit). Every part is named as it appears; only then is it scaled up. Naming a puzzle is not showing it (user, 30 Oct 2026).
- **A comparison is a story:** two things compared are one task done two ways, with a winner and a reason, never a table read aloud (user, 27 Oct 2026).
- **Mechanism, not history:** how it works now, on the technical level; how it came to be only when the origin is the mechanism (user, 28 Oct 2026).

## For the ear

- Sentences of 6 to 16 words, one idea each, joined by "because", "so", "but". Numbers and acronyms written as spoken ("G P U"). No exclamation marks.
- **The loop ending:** read the last sentence into the first; if it plays as one sentence, it passes. No "like and subscribe". A closing question, if any, can be answered in one word.
- **Script cues** (marked before the beat sheet; each gets a picture, never a caption). A number gets a picture built from that number (counted dots, a bar of that length). An abstract noun gets a concrete object from the viewer's world, never an AI symbol. A contrast word ("but", "instead") gets a turn in the picture.
- **Every setup is answered on screen:** a prompt typed, a question asked or a run started is followed by its result in the picture.
- **Cut test:** delete each sentence in turn; if the video still works, it stays deleted.

## Persona and banned words

- An engineer who uses AI every day and explains it to a smart friend: precise, dry, calm, never hype or doom. Specific beats general ("Claude Opus 5.5, October 2026", not "AI").
- **Banned** (voice, screen, description): did you know, game-changing, revolutionary, mind-blowing, insane, unlock, supercharge, journey, this changes everything, in this video, like and subscribe, seamless, reimagined, superpowers, the future of, meet (as an intro); exclamation marks. Copy shapes that read as machine-written: "Not just X, it's Y", triplets of one-word sentences ("Faster. Smarter. Cheaper."), a line that would fit any other video unchanged.

## Rigour

Every number has a source and date in the Script note. Every AI claim names model, version and date. The narrator describes mechanisms, never wants ("it predicts the next word", not "it thinks"). Recorded runs are real; speed-ups are marked; no keys or personal data appear.
