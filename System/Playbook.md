# Playbook: every rule for a Short

Read it whole before writing anything. **[H] marks a hypothesis** (a number or habit not yet tested on channel data); the 10-video review in [[Publish]] §Analytics loop turns each into a kept, changed or deleted rule. Model to imitate: [[Model script]]. One note, no history: when feedback changes a rule, **edit the rule here** ([[Feedback loop]]). Process, models and prices: [[Workflow]]. Publishing and numbers: [[Publish]]. Look and brand: [[Style]].

**Product:** vertical Shorts (9:16) for YouTube and TikTok, English, **five a week at a fixed time, never two on one day** (YouTube diagnostic, 9 Oct 2026: *"a steady, predictable schedule"*). Goal: **teach people AI**: how it works, how to put agents to work, and what it changes in the world, as narrated learning you enjoy even knowing it is made with AI. Same master on both platforms.

## 1. Idea (gate P1; read `Analytics/Report.md` first)

1. **Facts bank:** ≥ 15 remarkable true facts (mechanism, strange behaviour, scale, connections, expert details; news last), each with source and date. AI moves fast: a fact about what a model or product can do is ≤ 12 months old or dated on screen.
2. **Score** each angle 1 to 5 on surprise, scale, depth, evidence, showable, plain. **The thread must score ≥ 4 on surprise, scale and plain.** Show only the chosen row and one line per rejected candidate. **Argue against it:** a second pass lists the three strongest reasons a viewer would scroll past; if any is not answered by the thread, rescore it lower. Scores are the agent's guess until Report data exists. Report data: a hook type or topic that held `viewed_pct`, `shares`, `subs_gained` earns a bonus; one that fell is not repeated without a new angle (under 10 videos with data it guides, it does not decide).
3. **Street-lamp test (it has a fail state):** if a headline or product spec answers the central question, go back to 1. **"Borderline" is a fail:** either change the angle, or write one sentence saying which mechanism beyond the headline the video adds and get the user's yes before P2. Never record "borderline" and proceed. **Expert test:** a specialist would call it "the interesting part"; each scene has one detail a generic explainer skips. **Coffee test:** the thread in one plain sentence; a friend saying "so what?" means surprise ≤ 3.
4. **Five candidate hooks of different types;** pick with the **hook test** in §2 (not by length). The hook type (surprising fact, surprising number, question, contradiction, stakes) goes in the Status note and `Analytics/videos.csv`.
5. **Original synthesis:** one derived comparison or number per video (monetization policy: no template content).
6. **Demand check (outside evidence):** one line per finalist: is the subject searched or shared? Search autocomplete for the plain question, and the views of 3 comparable Shorts or videos (`youtube_search.py`, [[Audience]]). No demand for the mechanism = **bridge it to an outcome people already want**; no demand at all and a strong thread = write why it still earns a slot. Record it in the Status note.
8. **Outcome and mechanism, niche** ([[Audience]]): every idea names Y (the result the viewer wants) and X (the mechanism they did not know blocked it), sits in one of the three AI series, and was chosen after a deep-first search of the niche (`Pipeline/Niche map.md`).
7. **Topic spread and mini-series [H]** (YouTube diagnostic, 9 Oct 2026: *"cluster your topics into related mini-series"*): every video belongs to one series, **How it works**, **Agent at work** or **What's coming** ([[Audience]]), written in `videos.csv` (`series`), so a viewer of one finds the next. Rotate: two consecutive videos never share a series. Each series video ends its description with the next two titles of the series (in the Upload note), and the pinned comment links the previous one.

## 2. Script (gate P3)

- **Length in words, never seconds:** 130 to 190, aim 130 to 160, 6 to 8 scenes [H]. **Compact test [H]** (YouTube diagnostic, 9 Oct 2026: 6 in 10 viewers swipe away, long Shorts need tighter momentum; *"test 30 to 45 seconds"*): every third video is a compact Short of 80 to 115 words, 4 to 5 scenes, ≤ 3 facts, 4 to 7 plates; mark `compact` in `videos.csv` `series` or Status so the report can compare its `viewed_pct` and `avg_pct_viewed` with the long ones. Never pad a thread to reach 130; if the cut test leaves 100 words, ship 100. Plan 160 words ≈ 60 s as a guess; report duration only after the voice exists ([[Playbook]] §5).
- **One question, one theme line** (plain words, stated in scene 1 or 2). **Every scene opens with a bridge** back to it ("so", "that is why", "the catch"); the close answers it. **≤ 5 facts, ≤ 3 spoken figures.** One new idea per scene.
- **Scene 1 = the hook: a need-to-know claim a stranger understands at once.** **The first words are the twist itself** (the surprising fact or broken expectation), with no setup clause, greeting or context before it; the first clause is spoken inside 2 s (check it on the voice take, not by counting), because the swipe decision happens in the first 1 to 2 s (YouTube diagnostic, 9 Oct 2026: the best opener held 52.6 % viewed against a 39.7 % average). **Hook test, all five must hold:** (1) **Plain:** no term that a later scene defines ("A chatbot never looks anything up. It guesses.", not "autoregressive sampling"). (2) **Reason to care before the reaction:** it names a consequence for the viewer, a broken expectation, or a stake; a big number alone is not a hook. (3) **Open question:** a curious friend would ask "why?" or "how?", and the next scene answers it. (4) **Sound-off:** the first caption word plus the first image already raise that question. (5) **Honest:** the video delivers exactly what it promises (no malicious clickbait). (6) **Matches the package:** it restates the title and thumbnail promise (§2b), so the viewer feels they chose right; scene 2 then gives the **proof** and the **plan** ([[Audience]]). No date opener, "Imagine", greeting, "did you know", or question as the first words. Resolved within its scene or the next.
- **Low-attention-proof** (user, 9 Oct 2026: *"i got lost before 5 seconds... i saw no conclussion. content should be low attention proof"*): assume a viewer half-listening with the sound low. **The first sentence is one plain claim of ≤ 12 words with no technical term; the stake follows in the second.** No jargon in the voice that a later scene must define (parameter counts, benchmark names, model version strings): keep it on screen or cut it. One idea per sentence, ≤ 12 words. **The conclusion is spoken as a conclusion** ("So here is the point: ...") and shown on a card, **before** the social question, so a viewer who joined late still leaves with one sentence. Repeat the thread word (for example *guess* and *check*) in the hook, the middle and the conclusion.
- **Explain, do not narrate:** mechanism, cause → effect joined by "because / so / but", never "and then". Cut side detail, never the why. Concrete beats abstract. Facts answer "so what changes?" in at least two scenes.
- **Structure:** 1 hook · 2 minimal context (16 to 26) · 3 to 5 causal chain (20 to 30 each) · 6 turn (20 to 30) · 7 close (15 to 25).
- **Close = concrete payoff:** one plain sentence that answers the question and says what it means for something the viewer knows; repeatable to a friend. No aphorism, moral or summary. **Then one social question** (user, 6 Oct 2026: *"end on a way to fidelize the user, make him think, ask him something, make it social"*): a debate the viewer can answer in the comments ("Would you let an agent send email in your name?") plus a plain invitation ("Tell me what you think."); never "like and subscribe". Put the question on screen as a margin note. If it fits, the last plate is the first (loop).
- **Cut test:** delete each sentence in turn; if the video still answers its question, it stays deleted.
- **For the ear:** sentences of 6 to 16 words, active verbs, varied length, ≤ 1 rhetorical question, no exclamation marks, **the voice should be calm and unhurried, human enough to fall asleep to** (user, 5 Oct 2026). **Pause plan:** every boundary and deliberate beat is planned from the sentence's intention and emotion and written into the take; see the table below. A **delivery note per scene** (calm, rising, amused, grave). Figures and dates go on screen, not in the voice. Numbers and acronyms are written as spoken if the model misreads them ("G P U", "Claude" stays a word).
- **AI rigor:** every capability claim names the model, version and date it was true for ("GPT-5 in August 2025", never "AI can"); benchmarks are named with their source and caveat; forecasts are labelled as forecasts with who made them ("Epoch AI projects"), never stated as fact; describe models by mechanism, not intent ("it predicts the likely next word", not "it wants" or "it lies" without the mechanism); no hype words, no doom framing; a demo claim is shown as a real run (§3).
- **Rigor:** every figure has source and date; weak facts attributed or cut ("could"); convicted ≠ accused ≠ investigated; if sources conflict, say "a base recipe from several sources"; respect the focus the user fixed; the research note says what was not verified.
- **Pause plan [H]** (user, 5 Oct 2026: *"pauses should be paired with intention and emotionality of the phrase... the narrative rhythm so human that you could fall asleep listening"*). Since 9 Oct 2026 the pauses are **written into the one take** (§5), never cut into the audio: the sentence and paragraph structure gives the natural pauses, and only the deliberate beats are marked:

  | Where | How it is written | Silence (Inworld TTS-2, measured 9 Oct 2026) |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | The next scene continues the same thought (starts with "and", "so", "but") | no mark; the scene break is a paragraph | about 0.35 s |
  | A new idea | a full stop; a new scene is a new paragraph | 0.35 to 0.6 s |
  | Before the payoff or the central question (1 or 2 per video) | `beat` 0.2 to 0.3 after the scene, or `pauses` `[["word", 0.3]]` inside it: a `<break>` that **adds** to the natural pause | 0.7 to 0.9 s; never over 0.9 s |
  | After a short setup ("Simple, but slow.") | split it into its own sentence | natural |
  | **Never** | between subject and verb, after "the", "a", "of", "and", before a number's unit | the uncanny pause the user heard |

  The agent cannot hear: it writes the intention of each pause in the script table ("gentle", "let it land", "breath"), checks the take's silences against this table (`silencedetect`), and the user's listening rubric at P7 decides ([[Workflow]]). A pause the voice made inside a clause is fixed by rewording or punctuation and a retake, never by cutting.
- **Script table columns:** scene · exact voice text · words · delivery note · **pause intention and any deliberate beat (word, seconds)** · visual beat (what the viewer sees happen, medium) · on-screen text · source. The plate-level detail goes in the beat sheet (§3).

## 2b. Package (written inside the Script note, gate P3)

The Script note has a **Package** section before the scene table (user, 6 Oct 2026: thumbnail belongs to the script phase). Fields: **Y** and **X** (one line each) · **promise** (one plain sentence the video must pay) · **3 titles** (≤ 100 characters, recommended one marked) · **thumbnail**: one focal image (the anchor in its surprising state, drawn in the video's look) and at most 3 to 4 words that complement the title, never repeat it ([[Workflow]] §Thumbnail), and one line on **why it beats 3 neighbours in search** · **opening map**: scene 1 = promise, scene 2 = proof and plan. These fields are what `publish.json` later copies (`thumb`); changing the hook means re-checking the package. Rules: [[Audience]].

**Checklist before P3:** package written and promise matches scene 1 · facts bank and scores · street-lamp (not borderline), expert and against-the-idea tests · demand check · five hooks of different types · words in range · scene 1 passes the hook test · theme line and bridges · facts ≤ 5 · cut test · concrete ending · understandable without the images · read aloud, no drag · every claim sourced.

## 3. Visuals (gate P4a: the beat sheet, one row per plate)

Redesigned 9 Oct 2026 (user: *"Plates and additional stock images are not helping make the content easier to understand, that makes the short boring ... redesign the way you decide which photos to use, where put them, what kind of support content you use and how you display the elements"*); 3D plates and recorded runs added 23 Oct 2026 for the AI channel (user: *"choose new tools for 3d animation and more interesting visual rendering"*). Every rule here is [H] until the 4-against-4 comparison in [[Publish]] §Analytics loop.

- **Show the verb, not the noun.** Each sentence gets a **visual beat**: what the viewer must *see happen* at that moment to understand it with the sound off. **Usefulness test:** "the viewer sees ___ happen, so they understand ___". A beat that only shows a thing the voice names is allowed once, as the first look at a part the mechanism needs.
- **Swap test (decoration fails):** if the picture could be replaced by any other picture of the same noun with nothing lost, cut it (a glowing brain for "AI", a robot for "an agent", a server room for "the cloud"). A push on a generic photo is decoration, not motion.
- **Medium by what the sentence does** (first row that fits wins):

  | The sentence... | Medium |
  | --- | --- |
  | shows an AI or agent doing a task | a **recorded run**: real screen capture of the real tool doing it (`video`, cut to 9:16 on the action) |
  | shows a real thing working, a real place, a scale people know | **footage** of it doing the verb (`video`): a real data center, a real GPU, a real robot; a photo with a `move` to the named detail only when no footage exists |
  | explains a process inside a model with depth, space or many parts (tokens flowing through layers, an embedding space, attention links) | **3D plate** (`scene3d.py`): a faithful model of the mechanism, lit and moving as the voice says it |
  | explains a flat hidden process (a loop, a pipeline, a step-by-step) | **motion** plate (`motion.py`): a faithful schematic that moves as the voice says it |
  | gives a number | the number **as a comparison that builds** (a bar grows, a counter runs, unit dots fill); never a figure alone on a card |
  | describes change over time | a **chart that draws itself** on the words |
  | names a place or a route | an animated **map** (globe to region, pin, arc) |
  | weighs two things or a trade-off | both **in one frame** (split, switch, before and after) |

- **3 to 5 visual worlds and one anchor.** A Short uses a few worlds (the chat window, the row of tokens, the embedding space), not a string of unrelated pictures. The **anchor** (the everyday object of the hook) returns at the close and has changed state each time it returns. One source on more than 2 plates without a new state is a repeat (the build prints `REPEAT`).
- **Cut for a new step, move within it.** A plate starts **on the word** that opens a new step (`at`; the picture lands 0.15 s before the word). Within a step, a **visual event every 2 to 3 s** (a part enters, a label lands on it, a line draws, the camera moves to the detail named); nothing on screen stays unchanged for over 3.5 s (`motion.py` warns); a still holds ≤ 4 s. Count events, not plates: about 15 to 25 events on 6 to 12 plates per 160 words.
- **Parts build in the order they are named;** nothing labelled is visible before it is spoken. Scene 1 or 2 shows and names the one or two parts the mechanism needs.
- **One focal point:** the subject is copper or moving, everything else grey or still; on screen at once: the subject, at most one label or figure, the caption.
- **Real first for real things, drawn for hidden ones.** ≥ 60 % of the runtime moves (footage, motion plates or a purposeful `move`; the build prints `MOTION SHARE`); at least one real clip or photo when one shows the subject doing the verb.
- **Footage:** license PD, CC0, CC BY, CC BY-SA, Pexels or Pixabay only (`stock_videos.py` logs the credit at download); look at the frames before choosing (`look`); cut 3 to 5 s around the action with the focus on the subject (`cut --focus`); a 1080p landscape source crops to 608 px wide, so never for text or fine detail; no visible brand, logo, burned-in text or identifiable person unless they are the subject. A photo of a place shows it as it was in the story, or it is left out.
- **Recorded runs** (the Agent at work series lives on them): a real session of the real tool, recorded at a large font in a tall window, the prompt visible before the result; cut to the action, speed-ups marked on screen ("4x"), nothing staged or retyped after the fact; no API key, email, file path or personal data visible (blur or re-run); a product's interface is shown as itself for commentary, never imitated by a drawn mock-up.
- **3D plates** (`scene3d.py`, look in [[Style]] 3D plates): same ground, palette, lanes and word cues as motion plates; the camera moves slowly toward the part being named; ≤ 30 s of 3D per Short [H] (about 0.75 s a frame here, so about 10 minutes to render); render `--preview` while staging and look at the `--sheet` frames before the build.
- **AI clichés are banned** (they fail the swap test and mark a channel as generic): glowing brains, humanoid robots at keyboards, blue faces made of circuits, binary or Matrix rain, "AI" chip renders, handshakes between a human and a robot hand. Show the actual thing: the text a model wrote, the tokens, the run, the data center.
- **Motion plates** (`motion.py`, look and specs in [[Style]] Motion plates): drawn on the navy ground, ≤ 3 colours plus grey, text ≥ 40 px, nothing in the caption band or the interface zones (the renderer warns), every event on a word cue. Look at the `--sheet` frames of every plate before the build.
- **Generated media: opt-in, ≤ 3 per Short in all.** Stills: `openai/gpt-image-2`, **no custom style** (no clay, sketch, "cinematic", mood, props), content-first prompts in this order: subject (exact name) · structure (parts, count, arrangement, from the research note) · view · what must be readable (paraphrase, never the narration) · what each of 1 to 4 real references supplies · "plain background, no letters, numbers or labels". AI image-to-video (`lightricks/ltx-2.5-fast`, [[Workflow]]) only to add gentle motion to a checked real photo: never for a mechanism, an event that did not happen or a person; a photorealistic clip gets the AI disclosure ([[Publish]]).
- **Scientific fidelity beats creativity:** a schematic or animation shows the established model, only what the research note supports; prompt or draw what must not appear (no glow standing for physics, no invented parts); mark "schematic" on screen or in Status when simplified; an unsourced element is removed. Image models restyle diagrams: real diagrams are references or framed photos, never sources to redraw.
- **No bare backdrops, immersive plates** (user, 5 Oct 2026: *"sometimes the screen feels empty, only with a picture and a note"*): a figure or card sits on the thing it is about (footage, a photo or a motion plate), never on an empty gradient (the builder refuses `blank` plates). The subject fills the frame: footage cover-fitted, schematics drawn for 9:16. Legacy `contain` and `cutout` stills need `"bg": "blur"` or an inset (`EMPTY PLATE`).
- **Lanes (9:16):** subject in the upper lane (y 10 to 45 %), captions at 50 %, one supporting element (figure, label or card) in the lower lane (56 to 74 %); the bottom 25 % and the right 12 % belong to the interface ([[Style]]).
- **Mute test:** every beat makes sense with the sound off (the caption word plus the picture).
- **Beat sheet (P4a), one row per plate:** scene · `at` word · medium and source (file, or the search to run) · what the viewer sees happen (usefulness sentence) · events on which words · text on screen.

## 4. On-screen text (specificity test)

- **Purpose of each text:** a label names a part, sits on it and lands on the word that names it (1 to 4 words, ≥ 40 px, ≤ 2 on screen at once) · a figure is shown as a comparison that builds, with its unit and what it measures (§3); its source goes on screen or in the description · context card frames and sources (label = place/date/person/mechanism, title = a statement with a verb ≤ 26 characters, detail = source, scale or caveat ≤ 40) · margin note gives a voice (≤ 2 per video, ≤ 60 characters).
- **Specificity test:** could this text appear unchanged in a video about another topic? Then rewrite it with a name, number, place or mechanism. Banned: "The takeaway", "Key point", "Fun fact", "Same material", "Why it matters", "In summary" and the banned words in [[Style]]. Do not repeat the narration: captions are the only verbatim text. Cards are rare: the conclusion, the closing question, and a claim no picture can carry.
- **Symbols:** never write ≈ ≥ ≤ → in a card or callout (the font shows boxes): use "about", "or more", "to", "·". Figures like `180 °C`, `72.5 %`, `1,000`. Card captions ≤ 40 characters.
- **Word-by-word captions on every video**, from the voiced words (spelling for the eye in `captions`).
- **No branding inside the video** (no progress bar, mark, series tag, logo).

## 5. Voice and sound

Redesigned 9 Oct 2026 (user: *"voice and audio feels broken now, i want you to redesign that segment of the process"*; *"go for a higher tier model"*).

- **Voice:** `inworld/realtime-tts-2` on Replicate, voice `Dennis` (the user's choice, 9 Oct 2026), **one continuous take for the whole script** made by `voice.py`: the scenes are paragraphs of one request, so the prosody never resets and nothing is stitched. Settings: `speaking_rate` 0.95, `temperature` 0 (the model's default), 48 kHz WAV, one bracketed direction at the top: *"[say calmly and warmly, like a curious specialist explaining something to a friend]"* (the voice should be **calm and unhurried, human enough to fall asleep to**, user, 5 Oct 2026). A scene `delivery` direction only where the tone must change, sparingly. **Pace:** about 170 to 180 wpm overall (recognition is best near 170 for dense content, Rodero 2016); measured 177 on the first take. **Breaks:** ≤ 2 per video and each ≤ 0.3 s, because Inworld adds them to its own pause (§2). **Text:** plain spoken sentences, no digit inside a sentence, numbers and acronyms written as spoken; a word the model mispronounces, clips or flattens (user, 6 Oct 2026: "harmless", "one hundred", "target") is fixed by rewording around it, then a retake (one retake is normal, more needs a reason in Status); so is a word whisper hears as another word, because a half-listening viewer will too ("can halve a number" heard as "have", 9 Oct 2026). **Check the take** before building: the words whisper heard equal the script (no direction read aloud), every silence 0.3 to 0.9 s and none inside a clause. Prices and the model's limits: [[Workflow]]. The agent cannot hear: declare it at P7. Retired 9 Oct 2026: ElevenLabs turbo-v2.5 `Austin` per-scene takes with text context, and the builder's pause cutting (both caused what the user heard as broken).
- **Sound:** voice first. The builder adds only **the opening bell/thump the user likes** and a soft whoosh on each scene change (it falls in the pause between scenes); nothing fires under speech (ticks, pings and clicks are opt-in, `"events": "full"`), and the sine drone is gone (phones cannot play it). **Music is opt-in** (a plan row with source, license and purpose, approved at P4a): instrumental, no vocals, a calm minimal or ambient bed of 85 to 110 BPM with no lead melody in the voice band, set `lu` 15 LU under the voice and ducked about 9 dB while the voice speaks (about 23 LU under speech) by `mix_audio.py`. Sources in order: **Pixabay music chosen and downloaded by hand, shield-free tracks only** (a shield marks Content ID registration; Pixabay's API has no audio: 403 on 9 Oct 2026, and its terms forbid scraping) → Mixkit → YouTube Audio Library (CC BY tracks if cross-posted to TikTok) → Incompetech (CC BY, credit) → ccMixter (CC BY, credit); credits in `sound/_credits.jsonl`. **Never:** BBC/RemArc (non-commercial), CC BY-NC, game rips, unread licenses (NCS, BenSound, FMA). **BigSoundBank and Freesound are deferred**; no ML-generated sound unless the user approves it. Music against no music is an A/B for the analytics loop [H].
- **Mix and master** (`mix_audio.py`, after every render): the voice through a chain for phone speakers (high-pass 80 Hz, −2 dB at 250 Hz, +2.5 dB at 3.2 kHz, de-esser, 2.5:1 compression), music ducked under it, effects at their designed level, then a two-pass master to **−14 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP** (YouTube plays at −14 and never turns a quiet upload up; the old renders were −20.8). The script replaces the render's sound and prints the `MIX` line; the Blender render is kept in `render/_fx/`.

## 6. Edit (Blender 5.2, `build_video.py` + `plan.json`)

- The take is never cut: the builder pads the take (0.30 s before, 0.40 s after, 20 ms fade-in, 50 ms fade-out), places it once, ends each scene halfway through the silence before the next (from the heard words), and `CHECK=1` verifies lead-in, tail and overlaps. Caption and cue times start where the speech starts (whisper's early starts are snapped to the audio); a word heard for under 2 frames shares its caption with the next.
- Speech starts at once (`start` ≤ 0.2 s), the hook's visual claim (the anchor already doing the surprising thing, as footage or a motion plate; no title slide or logo) and first caption word on frame 1 (**the opening bell and the voice-first start are liked by the user: keep them**); the pauses are in the take (§2, §5; legacy plans with one take per scene still use `gaps` and `pauses`), 1.2 s tail; page turn between scenes, 0.2 s cross-fade within, 8 % push alternating in and out on stills; plates start on their `at` word (§3). The build prints `PLATES`, `LONG PLATE` (a still over 4 s, footage over 6 s), `REPEAT` and `MOTION SHARE`: fix before rendering. Motion plates: build once with `SOLO_PROYECTO=1` (writes `render/_timeline.json`), run `motion.py --sheet`, look at the sheets, then build and render.
- `plan.json` fields and builder details: header of `System/scripts/build_video.py`. Captions timed by `word_timings.py` (whisper, ≈ $0.01 per take), estimated if skipped; `render/<prefix>.srt` is written for upload. After the render, `mix_audio.py` mixes and masters the sound (§5).
- Blender 5.2 gotchas: `image_settings.media_type = "VIDEO"` before `FFMPEG`; `sequence_editor.strips`; `new_effect(length=...)`; `keyframe_new_interpolation_type = "LINEAR"`; `view_transform = "Standard"`; absolute paths in the `.blend`; TEXT default font is monospaced (load Noto Sans); system Python has no numpy (Blender's does); background mixdown is silent (the builder checks strips).

## 7. Checklist before P7 (the agent runs it and says what it could not check)

- [ ] Scene 1 passes the five-part hook test; first clause spoken inside 2 s on the real take; speech ≤ 0.2 s after frame 1
- [ ] Captions on from the first word (`CAPTIONS n words` printed), stills show no overlap with cards or callouts
- [ ] Every beat shows its verb (usefulness and swap tests), no AI cliché; every motion and 3D sheet and footage `look` was looked at; recorded runs show no key or personal data; every generated plate checked against the research note
- [ ] No bare gradient plate; `MOTION SHARE` ≥ 60 %, no `REPEAT`, no `WARN` from `motion.py`, no still over 4 s
- [ ] Theme line bridged by every scene, facts ≤ 5, concrete ending; every AI capability claim carries model, version and date
- [ ] Every on-screen figure sourced (card or description); no in-video branding
- [ ] Audio: one take; whisper heard the script's words; no silence over 0.9 s or inside a clause; `MIX` at −14 LUFS (±1) and true peak ≤ −1 dBTP
- [ ] **Not verified, said out loud:** how the voice sounds, caption timing against speech, sound levels, pace in motion. P7 belongs to the user.
