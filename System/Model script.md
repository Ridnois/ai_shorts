## Annotated scenes

| Scene | Voice | Why it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | "AI will invent a source, then swear it is real. In twenty twenty-three, New York lawyers filed court cases ChatGPT had invented. A judge fined them." | The twist is the first words; plain; a stake (a fine) and a real, dated case as **proof** |
| 2 | "It happens because AI does not look sources up. It predicts the words that probably come next, and a citation is easy to imitate: names, a title, a year." | The **mechanism** in plain words, joined by **because**; describes what the model does, not what it "wants" |
| 4 | "Here is the five second check. Copy the exact title, put it in quotes, and search it." | Pays the promise with something the viewer can **do** today (the Agent at work habit inside a How it works video) |

Every scene answers the question the previous one raised ([[Playbook]] §2 Connection test). Nothing is narrated ("and then"); each sentence is a cause, an effect or a step. How it works videos run hook, definition, origin, explanation on one running example, turn, close.

## The arc in one script ([[Script - Chatbots agree with you]])

| Beat | Words | Job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook (contradiction, 11 words) | "ChatGPT got so agreeable that OpenAI took its own update back." | the twist first, a real recall as the stake; opens loop 1 |
| Cause | "Nobody told it to flatter anyone. The update added one new reward: thumbs-up clicks." | proof and plan; opens loop 2 (why would a thumb flatter?) |
| Answer, at 43 % | "People click thumbs-up on answers that agree with them. So a model trained for thumbs-up learns that agreeing wins." | closes loops 1 and 2 in one plain sentence |
| Turn, at 54 % | "But this is not one bad update." | reverses "a one-off bug"; a re-hook with a new number (five of five) |
| Test, then result | "Ask one question twice… Llama 3 scored it four both times." | a check the viewer can run, run live, result shown as it came out |
| Loop-back | "A chatbot that always agrees is a mirror, not a checker. Which is yours?" | re-asks the hook in a new light; the last frame is frame 0, level |

## Hook rewrites (weak to strong)

Judged by the five-part hook test in [[Playbook]] §2, not by length. Shapes to imitate, not verified facts: check every claim against the research note.

| Weak | Why weak | Stronger | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| "The idea that trains every AI chatbot dates from 1847." (Gradient descent r1) | An abstract subject ("the idea"), no stake, and it starts the history before the viewer wants it (user, 23 Oct 2026) | "Every AI model learns with one old idea: gradient descent. Here is how it works." | Subject named, a stake (every model), the promise of the how; then definition, origin (Cauchy, 1847), explanation on one running example |
| "Large language models use attention." | A term, no stake, no question a stranger would ask | "ChatGPT reads your whole message before every single word it writes." | A broken expectation in plain words; opens "why would it do that?", which the video answers (attention) |
| "AI agents are the next big thing." | Hype, no claim, fails the specificity test | "An AI agent can finish your task and still break the rule you gave it." | A stake for anyone who uses agents; opens "how?"; the video shows a real run |
| "AI uses a lot of electricity." | Vague, already known | "One AI data center can draw as much power as a city." | Scale plus surprise; needs the named facility, figure and date before it is used |

## Before you pick a hook

Say it aloud to a friend who uses ChatGPT but has never read about it. If they say "so?", it fails the test. If they ask "why?" or "how do I...?", keep it.
